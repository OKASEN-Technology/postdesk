I bake three loaves most weekends and write down what worked. This time
the question was the rest: does a long, cold fermentation change the crumb
enough to be worth the fridge space?

## The setup

Three loaves from one batch of dough at 78% hydration. One went into the
oven after 12 hours on the counter, one after 36 hours in the fridge, and
one after 72 hours.

## What changed

The 72-hour loaf had the most open crumb of the three and a crust that
blistered all over, the way a bakery loaf does. The 36-hour loaf was close.
The 12-hour loaf was fine, and dull next to the others.

The longer rest also made the dough easier to score: cold and firm, it
took a clean cut where the warm dough dragged.

## What to do with that

If you have the fridge space, give the dough three days. If you do not,
36 hours gets you most of the way. The full method, with timings, is at
[the link](https://example.com/bakes/three-loaves).
